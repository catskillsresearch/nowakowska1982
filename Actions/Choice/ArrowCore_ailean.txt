A three-option ranking (`Ranking3`) assigns each option a rank value in
$\{0,1,2\}$. The induced total preorder (`rankingPreorder`) compares options by
numerical rank. A two-scale profile (`Profile2`) is a pair of rankings; a social
welfare function (`SWF2`) maps profiles to social rankings.

Scale $i$ is a dictator (`IsDictator`) when every strict preference of that
scale is copied into the social ranking. Lack of a dictator (`LackOfDictator`)
means neither scale is dictatorial. Unanimity (`Unanimity`) requires that shared
strict preferences of both scales appear socially. Independence of irrelevant
alternatives (`IIA`) requires that the social comparison of a pair depend only
on the two scales’ comparisons of that same pair. Sovereignty (`Sovereignty`)
requires that for every distinct pair of options, each orientation of the social
strict order is realized by some profile.
