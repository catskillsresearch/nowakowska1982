The pure first-scale social welfare function (`scale1Dictator`) returns the
first ranking of the profile.

**Theorem** (`scale1Dictator.isDictator`). The first scale is a dictator.
*Proof.* Social strict preferences are exactly those of the first scale.

**Theorem** (`scale1Dictator.unanimity`). Unanimity holds. *Proof.* A shared
strict preference of both scales is in particular a preference of the first
scale.

**Theorem** (`scale1Dictator.IIA`). Independence of irrelevant alternatives
holds. *Proof.* The social comparison of a pair is the first scale’s comparison,
which is assumed invariant between the two profiles.

**Theorem** (`scale1Dictator.not_lack`). Lack of a dictator fails. *Proof.* The
first scale is a dictator.

**Theorem** (`scale1Dictator.sovereignty`). Sovereignty holds. *Proof.* For
distinct $a,b$, the profile that prefers $a$ to $b$ on both scales (resp.\ $b$ to
$a$) yields the corresponding social orientation.
