**Nowakowska, Theory of Actions (1982), pp. 156–157**

An expression is inadmissible if there is no context in which it may be
meaningfully applied. Semantic implication $A\to_s B$ means that ‘$A$ and
not $B$’ is inadmissible. The motivational calculus consists of semantic
implications concerning sentences with motivational functors.

**Nowakowska, Language of Motivation and Language of Actions (1973), §2.1.4 (sampled)**

Among the rules of semantic inference:
$$
\begin{aligned}
(2)/(5)&\quad K(p\&q)\leftrightarrow Kp\& Kq,\\
(3)/(6)&\quad Cr(p\&q)\leftrightarrow Cr\,p\& Cr\,q,\\
(4)/(7)&\quad T(p\&q)\leftrightarrow Tp\& Tq,\\
(11)&\quad K'(p,q)\xrightarrow{s}\sim Kp,\\
(16)&\quad Cr\,p\xrightarrow{s}\sim T\,\sim p,\\
(18)&\quad Tp\xrightarrow{s}T'\,\sim p,\\
(22)&\quad T'p\& T'q\xrightarrow{s}T'(p\lor q),\\
(23)&\quad T'p\xrightarrow{s}\sim Tp,\\
(26)&\quad B'p\xrightarrow{s}T\,\sim p,\\
(30)&\quad Dp\xrightarrow{s}T'p,\\
(48)&\quad G(a,P)\xrightarrow{s}K_3(a,P)\lor T_3(a,P).
\end{aligned}
$$
