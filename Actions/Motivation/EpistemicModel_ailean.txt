An epistemic model (`EpistemicModel`) over a speaker-world $X$ (with
propositions as sets, `PropSet`) consists of a set-function $P$ together with
regions $v_T$, $v_B$, $v_{Cr}$, and $v_D$, subject to the nesting
$v_T\subseteq v_B\cap v_{Cr}\cap v_D$. The function $P$ normalizes the universe to
$1$ and the empty set to $0$, splits certainty under intersection
($P(p\cap q)=1$ iff both factors have $P=1$), and sends any set disjoint from a
certainty-$1$ set to probability $0$.

Knowledge of $\alpha$ (`Know`) means $P(\alpha)=1$. Certainty (`Certain`) means
$v_{Cr}\subseteq\alpha$. Thinking (`Think`) means $v_T\subseteq\alpha$; not-thinking
(`DontThink`) means $\alpha\subseteq v_T^c$. Belief (`Believe`) and its negation
(`DontBelieve`) are the analogous containments for $v_B$; doubt (`Doubt`) is
containment of $\alpha$ in $v_D^c$. Not-knowing-whether (`DontKnowWhether`)
between complementary $\alpha$ and $\beta$ means they partition the universe and
both receive strictly positive $P$.
