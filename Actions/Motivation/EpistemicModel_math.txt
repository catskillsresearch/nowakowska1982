**Nowakowska, Language of Motivation and Language of Actions (1973), §2.2.4, pp. 79–89**

Formally, the model is an ordered set of six objects
$$
\langle X, P, v_T, v_B, v_{Cr}, v_D \rangle
$$
with $v_T \subset v_B \cap v_{Cr} \cap v_D$. Propositional arguments are sets in
$X$; $\sim$, $\&$, $\lor$ on those arguments are complement, intersection, and
union. The functors are interpreted by
$$
\begin{aligned}
K\alpha &\equiv P(\alpha)=1, &
Cr\alpha &\equiv \alpha \supset v_{Cr}, &
T\alpha &\equiv \alpha \supset v_T, \\
T'\alpha &\equiv \alpha \subset v_T^{c}, &
B\alpha &\equiv \alpha \supset v_B, &
B'\alpha &\equiv \alpha \subset v_B^{c}, \\
D\alpha &\equiv \alpha \subset v_D^{c},
\end{aligned}
$$
and $K'(\alpha,\beta)$ when $\alpha\cap\beta=\emptyset$, $\alpha\cup\beta=X$, and both
have positive $P$-mass.
