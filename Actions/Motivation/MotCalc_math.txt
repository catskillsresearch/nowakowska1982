**Nowakowska, Language of Motivation and Language of Actions (1973), §2.2.4, pp. 79–89**

An epistemic model is a sixtuplet $\langle X,P,v_T,v_B,v_{Cr},v_D\rangle$ with
$v_T\subset v_B\cap v_{Cr}\cap v_D$. Functors are interpreted by
$Kp\equiv P(p)=1$, $Cr\,p\equiv v_{Cr}\subset p$, $Tp\equiv v_T\subset p$,
$T'p\equiv p\subset v_T^{c}$, and likewise for $B$, $B'$, $D$. Under this
reading the sampled semantic implications of §2.1.4 (e.g.
$K(p\&q)\leftrightarrow Kp\& Kq$, $T'p\& T'q\to T'(p\lor q)$,
$Cr\,p\to\sim T\sim p$, $Gp\to Kp\lor Tp$) become true set-theoretic
statements. Prefer/want/can appear as thin additional relations with the
cross-functor samples used in the library.
