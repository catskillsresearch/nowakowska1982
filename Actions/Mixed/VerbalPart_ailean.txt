An utterance occurrence (`VerbalOcc`) is a triple $(\alpha,u,v)$ consisting of a
sentence $\alpha$ together with a mixed prefix $u$ and suffix $v$. Reconstructing
the host string (`VerbalOcc.reconstruct`) yields $u\,\alpha\,v$. The behavioural
prefix and suffix are $\pi(u)$ and $\pi(v)$ (`piPrefix`, `piSuffix`). The
discrete time of the occurrence (`VerbalOcc.time`) is the sum, over letters of
$u$, of floor durations of actions and pause-unit durations of sentences
(`letterTime`). Wanted, unwanted, obligation, and prohibition sets at the
occurrence (`W_at`, `W'_at`, `O_at`, `O'_at`) are those of $\alpha$ evaluated at
that discrete time.

Scanning a mixed string from left to right produces the list of its successive
utterance occurrences (`verbalOccs`, via `verbalOccsAux`): each sentence letter
contributes an occurrence whose prefix is everything already seen and whose
suffix is the unread remainder; action letters merely extend the scanning
prefix.

**Theorem** (`verbalOccs.reconstruct`). Every occurrence produced by scanning a
string $x$ reconstructs to $x$. *Proof.* Strengthen to: every occurrence
recorded while scanning a remainder $r$ under an accumulated prefix $p$
reconstructs to $pr$. Induction on $r$: the empty remainder contributes nothing;
an action letter extends the prefix and the inductive hypothesis applies; a
sentence $\alpha$ either is the head occurrence (prefix $p$, suffix the rest),
which reconstructs as $p\,\alpha\,(\mathrm{rest})=pr$, or else appears later, in
which case the inductive hypothesis after extending the prefix by $\alpha$
applies. Starting the scan with empty prefix yields the claim for $x$.
