**Nowakowska, Theory of Actions (1982), pp. 144–145**

According to the above general intuition, the primitive concepts of the system may be taken as a sixtuplet

$$
\langle D, \#, \varphi, L, S, R \rangle
$$

(1)

with the following interpretation.

The set $D$ consists of elementary actions, with one action $\#$ distinguished, to be interpreted as ‘pause’ (any action which is outside the scope of interest for the description of a given situation being analysed). The function $\varphi$ represents the duration of an action, i.e. $\varphi(d)$ is the time needed to perform $d$. For simplicity it is assumed that $\varphi(\#)=1$, the longer pauses being represented by concatenations $\# \# \ldots \#$.

Composite actions will be, by definition, strings of elementary actions $d_1 d_2 \ldots d_n$. … Accordingly, in the class $D^*$ of all finite strings of elementary actions, one distinguishes those which are admissible in some specified sense ; these strings constitute, by definition, the language of actions $L$ so that $L$ is a subset of $D^*$.

… The pairs $(s, t)$, where $s \in S$ and $t$ is a moment of time, will be referred to as time-results … The symbol $uR(s, t)$, where $u \in L$, $s \in S$ and $t$ is a time moment means that performing $u$ leads to the occurrence of $s$ at the time $t$.
