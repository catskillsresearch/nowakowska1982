**Nowakowska, Theory of Actions (1982), pp. 169–171**

The primitive notion here is an automaton, understood as a system

$$
\langle U, B, \Theta, D, \#, \tau, \pi \rangle
$$

… Formally, $\tau$ is a relation in the set $U \times B \times D$, with the property that for any $u$ and $b$ there exists $d$ such that $(u, b, d)$ is in $\tau$. …

… $\pi : U \times B \times D \to U$, and $\pi$ is defined only for those triplets $(u, b, d)$, for which $d \in \tau(u, b)$. …

… $d_1 d_2 \ldots d_n$ belongs to the language of actions, if …

$$d_{j+1} \in \tau(u_j, b_j),$$

where $u_j = \pi(u_{j-1}, b_{j-1}, d_j)$.
