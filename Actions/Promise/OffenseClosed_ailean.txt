Offense closure (`OffenseClosed`, condition (b)) asserts that if $y\in U$ and
the two-sided extension $xyz$ is admissible, then $xyz\in U$: breaking is
preserved under admissible embedding.

**Theorem** (`OffenseClosed.eq_L_inter_suffix`). If $U\subseteq L$ is
offense-closed, then $U=L\cap C$ where $C$ is the offense suffix language of $U$.
*Proof.* Every breaker has a first-offense prefix, hence lies in $C$.
Conversely, $x=uv$ with $u\in\hat U\subseteq U$ and $x\in L$ upgrades by closure
with empty left context.

**Theorem** (`OffenseClosed.isContextFree`, offense CF). If $U\subseteq L$ is
offense-closed with finite offense horizon, $L$ is context-free, and
context-free languages are closed under intersection with regular languages,
then $U$ is context-free. *Proof.* Write $U=L\cap C$ with $C$ regular, and apply
the classical intersection hypothesis. (Offense-primality is carried as an
additional hypothesis in the development but is not required for this
intersection argument.)
