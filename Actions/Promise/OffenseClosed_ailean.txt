Offense closure (`OffenseClosed`, condition (b)) asserts that if $y\in U$ and
the two-sided extension $xyz$ is admissible, then $xyz\in U$: breaking is
preserved under admissible embedding.

**Theorem** (`OffenseClosed.eq_L_inter_suffix`). If $U\subseteq L$ is
offense-closed, then $U=L\cap C$ where $C$ is the offense infix envelope of $U$.
*Proof.* Every breaker factors through a factor-minimal first offense in
$\hat U$, hence lies in $C$. Conversely, $x=avb$ with $v\in\hat U\subseteq U$ and
$x\in L$ upgrades by two-sided closure.

**Theorem** (`OffenseClosed.isContextFree`, offense CF). If $U\subseteq L$ is
offense-closed with finite offense horizon and $L$ is context-free, then $U$ is
context-free. *Proof.* Write $U=L\cap C$ with $C$ regular (infix envelope under
finite horizon), and apply the proved CF ∩ Regular theorem. Offense-primality
is not required for this argument and is not a hypothesis of the theorem.
