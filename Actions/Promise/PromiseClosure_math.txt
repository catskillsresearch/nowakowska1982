**Nowakowska, Theory of Actions (1982), pp. 159–160**

Let $T\subseteq L$ be the set of strings that *fulfill* a promise, and require
$$
xy\in L\ \text{and}\ x\in T\quad\Rightarrow\quad xy\in T.
$$
Let $\hat{T}$ be the *minimal fulfilling strings* (no nonempty proper prefix still in $T$), and define the *motivational horizon* by $h(T)=\sup\{\varphi(x):x\in\hat{T}\}$. For a set $U$ of *breaking* strings, require that if $xyz\in L$ and $y\in U$, then $xyz\in U$; write $\hat{U}$ for the *first offenses*. If $L$ is context-free and $h(T)$ is finite, then $T$ is context-free as well.

**Nowakowska, Language of Motivation and Language of Actions (1973), §5.3.2–5.3.3, pp. 204–210**

Assume $D_+^*$ is context-free. If $H_O(\alpha,k)<\infty$, then $O_{(\alpha,k)}$ is context-free (Thm 1): the suffix language $C=\{uv:u\in\bar O\}$ is regular by Myhill–Nerode (finite horizon ⇒ finitely many short non-members), and $O=D_+^*\cap C$. Dually, under primality (9) and finite max $\varphi$ on first offenses $\bar O'$, the set $O'_{(\alpha,k)}$ is context-free (Thm 4).
