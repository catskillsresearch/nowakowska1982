The promise suffix language of $T$ (`promiseSuffixLang`) consists of all strings
that begin with some minimal fulfiller from $\hat T$ (arbitrary continuation
afterward).

**Theorem** (`promiseSuffixLang.suffix_closed`). The language is closed under
appending an arbitrary suffix. *Proof.* If $x=uv$ with $u\in\hat T$, then
$xy=u(vy)$.

**Theorem** (`promiseSuffixLang.none_of_long`). If every minimal fulfiller has
duration at most $M$, and $x$ lies outside the suffix language with
$\varphi(x)>M$, then no extension $xy$ enters the language. *Proof.* Any
factorization through a minimal fulfiller $u$ would force either $x$ itself to
begin with a short enough minimal fulfiller (contradicting exclusion) or a
duration contradiction via additivity.

**Theorem** (`promiseSuffixLang.isRegular`). Under finite horizon (and a finite
nonempty alphabet), the promise suffix language is regular. *Proof.*
Myhill–Nerode: left quotients of strings fall into finitely many classes—those
already in the language (quotient universal), those longer than the horizon and
outside it (quotient empty), and those of duration at most the horizon
(finitely many such strings, each contributing one quotient).
