**Nowakowska, Theory of Actions (1982), pp. 151–152**

… in the set $R^{-1}(f(C))$ of all strings of actions which bring about the goal $f(C)$, one may distinguish the subsets $\mathrm{Prax}\,f(C)$ and $\mathrm{Prax\,min}\,f(C)$. The first consists of those strings which are not ‘economically dominated’, and lead to $f(C)$, i.e.

$$
\mathrm{Prax}\,f(C) = \{u \in R^{-1}(f(C));\ \text{there is no } u' \in R^{-1}(f(C))\ \text{with } u \prec u'\ \text{where } u \prec u'\ \text{if } u'\ \text{results from } u\ \text{by crossing out some of its actions and replacing them by } \#\}.
$$

$$
\mathrm{Prax\,min}\,f(C) = \{u \in \mathrm{Prax}\,f(C)\ :\ \text{there is no } u' \in \mathrm{Prax}\,f(C)\ \text{with } \varphi(u') < \varphi(u)\},
$$
