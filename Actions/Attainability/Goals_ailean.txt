A finite goal configuration $C$ (`GoalSet`) is a list of time-results. The weak
realizing set (`RinvUnion`) is the union of the individual inverse images; the
strong realizing set (`RinvInter`) is their intersection. Weak attainability
(`WeaklyAttainable`) means the union is nonempty (at least one time-result in
$C$ can be brought about); strong attainability (`StronglyAttainable`) means the
intersection is nonempty (some single string realizes every member of $C$).

**Theorem** (`strongly_implies_weakly`). If $C$ is nonempty and strongly
attainable, then it is weakly attainable. *Proof.* A common realizer of every
member of $C$ realizes, in particular, the first member, hence lies in the
union.

The negation of a time-result $(s,t)$ (`RinvNeg`) consists of the admissible
strings that do not cause $(s,t)$. Motive conflict (`MotiveConflict`) is the
pattern “weakly but not strongly attainable”: each desideratum is separately
achievable, but no string achieves them all at once.

**Theorem** (`weakly_iff_strongly_singleton`). On a singleton goal set
$\{(s,t)\}$, weak and strong attainability coincide. *Proof.* The union and
intersection of a one-element family are the same set, so nonemptiness is
equivalent.
