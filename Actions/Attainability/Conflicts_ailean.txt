When four labeled motives fail to be jointly strongly attainable, a conflict
pattern records which nonempty proper subsets remain strongly attainable.
Strong attainability is downward closed, so a pattern is an order ideal
(downset) in the Boolean lattice on four atoms that contains every singleton
and omits the full four-set.

With atoms fixed in and the top fixed out, a pattern is determined by which of
the six doubletons (`doubletons`) and four tripletons (`tripleRequirements`)
are included, subject to: a tripleton may appear only if all three of its
supporting doubletons do (`doubletonChosen`, `allowedTripleCount`). Summing,
over all $2^6=64$ choices of doubleton sets, the quantity $2^{k}$
(`patternsFor`) where $k$ is the number of tripletons allowed by that choice,
yields the total number of patterns (`conflictPatternCount`).

**Theorem** (`conflictPattern_card`). There are exactly $113$ such labeled
four-motive conflict patterns. *Proof.* The finite sum described above
evaluates to $113$ by exhaustive decision of the arithmetic.

As sanity checks: the empty doubleton choice allows no tripletons and
contributes $2^0=1$ pattern (`patternsFor_zero`); choosing all six doubletons
allows all four tripletons and contributes $2^4=16$ patterns
(`patternsFor_allDoubletons`). A Boolean recognizer on $16$-bit family masks
(`isDownset`, `hasAllAtoms`, `hasTop`, `isConflictPattern`) decides membership
of any given mask (`isConflictPattern_decide`).
